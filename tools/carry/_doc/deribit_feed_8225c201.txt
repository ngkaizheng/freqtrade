Before go-live, P99 latency was roughly 26× the median and swung from ∼80 ms in quiet hours to ∼600 ms at busy moments. After go-live, P99 is only about 3× the median and stays within a stable 180–420 <span style="font-family: Arial, sans-serif;">µ</span>s band.</p>
<h2>Latency percentiles</h2>
<p>Latency is often reduced to a single number, but an average only shows the typical experience, not what happens when the exchange is under pressure;</p>
<ul>
<li>Median (P50): the typical request-response time. Half of requests are faster, half are slower.</li>
<li>P90: the time within which 90% of requests complete.</li>
<li>P99: the time within which 99% of requests complete, highlighting the slowest 1%.</li>
</ul>
<p>For market makers and automated trading firms, these slower responses matter most. A delayed response can leave a quote active longer than intended or cause a system to act on outdated market data. By reducing these tail latencies, the new matching engine makes order handling more consistent, especially during high activity.</p>
<h2>What changed</h2>
<p>The new matching engine is designed for determinism and low latency. Order entry and market data now run on dedicated paths with fewer processing steps. Risk checks that used to sit on the critical path have been restructured where possible.</p>
<p>The Classic API remains fully supported. The new system trades breadth of functionality for speed and predictability.</p>
<p>The migration was completed incrementally while the Classic stack remained fully operational, allowing participants to move to the new order entry gateways at their own pace.</p>
<h2>Performance under load</h2>
<p>We analyzed the two heaviest days of the post window, 24 and 25 August, when the matching engine processed 1.02 billion events. Median latency rises just 10% from the quietest to the busiest conditions. Congestion shows up only in the tail.</p>
<p>The busiest single second, 25 August at 02:41:28 UTC, saw 23,836 requests. One 4.5 millisecond window alone contained 691 requests. Median latency rose to 609 µs against a 79 µs baseline, with the worst round trip just under 0.8 milliseconds. The backlog cleared within 2 milliseconds.</p>
<p>To rephrase: the largest spikes are now handled within the same timeframe that used to handle a single order.</p>
<h2>What this means for Deribit users</h2>
<p>For traders using the website or mobile app, no action is required. The upgrade improves the underlying trading environment without changing the user experience. When a new market microstructure is fully formed, these traders can expect tighter and deeper liquidity to trade against.</p>
<p>For brokers, technology providers, and firms operating at millisecond-level latency, Classic API connectivity remains available over the internet.</p>
<p>For institutional market makers and latency-sensitive participants, the new binary interfaces offer a direct, lower-overhead path to order entry and market data.</p>
<p>The main benefit is not simply speed. It is consistency: fewer extreme delays, stable performance under load, and a more predictable environment for automated trading strategies. More predictability allows the market to become more liquid by reducing execution uncertainty, helping participants manage adverse-selection risk, and improving hedging conditions.</p>
<p>Beyond the immediate latency improvements, the new matching engine gives Deribit the headroom to grow. With throughput no longer constrained by the bottlenecks of the previous system, we can list more products across more underlyings and instrument types without degrading performance for existing participants.</p>
<p>This scalability also supports features that were previously impractical at scale, such as broader cross-collateral adoption, which had limited uptake due to latency concerns.</p>

<h3>Block Scholes Risk Appetite (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>Block Scholes’ Risk Appetite index uses a composite spot index price; <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.10-index-prices" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/price/index</a></p>
<h3>1-month ATM implied volatility (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>At-the-money IV at a constant 1-month tenor; forward- looking vol expectations; <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.5-implied-volatility-by-moneyness" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/iv/moneyness</a></p>
<h3>Volatility term structure &amp; SVI ATM IV by tenor (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>SVI-fitted ATM IV across constant tenors (7d / 14d / 30d / 90d / 180d); <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.9-model-parameters" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/modelparams</a></p>
<h3>25-delta risk reversal / skew (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>Spread between 25Δ call and 25Δ put IV; a measure of upside vs downside option demand; <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.6-implied-volatility-by-risk-reversal-call-put-skew" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/iv/risk-reversal</a></p>
<h3>Market composite volatility surface (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>BlockScholes composite SVI surface across forward moneyness and tenor, aggregating market-wide options pricing into one clean fitted surface; <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.9-model-parameters" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/iv/moneyness + POST /api/v1/modelparams</a></p>
<h3>Cross-exchange volatility smiles (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>Venue-level (Deribit, Bybit) and composite smiles at a fixed tenor, for comparing exchange pricing against the market composite; <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.5-implied-volatility-by-moneyness" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/iv/moneyness</a></p>
<h3>Constant-maturity volatility smiles (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>Like-for-like smile at a fixed tenor across strikes, showing how the smile shifted between dates; <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.3-implied-volatility-by-strike" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/iv/strike</a></p>

<p>The pair discuss why the combination of huge government deficits, AI-related borrowing and pressure across global sovereign bonds may eventually force policymakers to inject significantly more liquidity into the system. With Treasury buybacks, potential TGA deployment, swap lines and other measures already being discussed, Dave argues that the real Bitcoin bull case emerges if these interventions have to be dramatically scaled up.</p>
<p>They then turn to Bitcoin’s recent breakout and the options market. Volatility initially jumped as BTC broke higher, but implied and realised volatility have quickly compressed again, while the bullish shift towards calls has largely faded back to neutral. With significant resistance around $80–85k and major macro events approaching, September could provide the next test of whether Bitcoin’s renewed uptrend has staying power.</p>
<p>Finally, Imran breaks down the actively managed Bitcoin hedging strategy he has been running over the past 18 months. Using risk reversals against part of his BTC holdings, the strategy has delivered roughly 70% upside participation versus 50% downside participation, while reducing annualised volatility from around 45% for Bitcoin to 25%.</p>

<p>The August summer volatility lull was significantly disrupted after an announcement from Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent that the US Treasury would at least double the size of its buyback program for off-the-run longer-dated government bonds. That announcement helped raise chatters of the so-called Debasement Trade and also coincided with a push from President Trump for Senate to pass the Clarity Act. Together, the events helped drive a 20% rally in BTC over the past week (with BTC now trading above $80K) and a 30% rally in ETH.</p>
<p>All three major directional measures of sentiment in derivatives markets repriced with the move in spot. Options skew is now firmly positive, and marks one of the few times this year that tenors beyond 7-days trade with a call-premium. Funding rates remain positive and in futures markets, the term structure is inverted.</p>

<h3>Block Scholes Risk Appetite (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>Block Scholes’ Risk Appetite index uses a composite spot index price; <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.10-index-prices" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/price/index</a></p>
<h3>1-month ATM implied volatility (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>At-the-money IV at a constant 1-month tenor; forward- looking vol expectations; <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.5-implied-volatility-by-moneyness" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/iv/moneyness</a></p>
<h3>Volatility term structure &amp; SVI ATM IV by tenor (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>SVI-fitted ATM IV across constant tenors (7d / 14d / 30d / 90d / 180d); <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.9-model-parameters" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/modelparams</a></p>
<h3>25-delta risk reversal / skew (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>Spread between 25Δ call and 25Δ put IV; a measure of upside vs downside option demand; <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.6-implied-volatility-by-risk-reversal-call-put-skew" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/iv/risk-reversal</a></p>
<h3>Market composite volatility surface (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>BlockScholes composite SVI surface across forward moneyness and tenor, aggregating market-wide options pricing into one clean fitted surface; <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.9-model-parameters" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/iv/moneyness + POST /api/v1/modelparams</a></p>
<h3>Cross-exchange volatility smiles (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>Venue-level (Deribit, Bybit) and composite smiles at a fixed tenor, for comparing exchange pricing against the market composite; <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.5-implied-volatility-by-moneyness" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/iv/moneyness</a></p>
<h3>Constant-maturity volatility smiles (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>Like-for-like smile at a fixed tenor across strikes, showing how the smile shifted between dates; <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.3-implied-volatility-by-strike" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/iv/strike</a></p>

This decision reflects changes to our wallet infrastructure as part of our ongoing integration with Coinbase.</p>
<h2>Proof of Reserves page</h2>
<p>Deribit introduced its <a href="https://www.deribit.com/statistics/BTC/proof-of-reserves" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Proof of Reserves page</a> in 2022 as a voluntary transparency initiative, following increased industry demand for greater visibility into exchange liabilities and reserves.</p>
<p>The page used a privacy-preserving binary Merkle tree. Client balances were split by asset and represented as hashed entries, allowing clients to verify which entries related to their accounts without exposing individual balances. The aggregate liabilities could then be compared with the on-chain balances of wallets published by Deribit.</p>
<p>This methodology and wallet structure were designed around Deribit’s existing wallet infrastructure. As we continue our integration with Coinbase, daily Proof of Reserves updates will no longer be published and the Proof of Reserves page will be removed.</p>
<h2>What this means for clients</h2>
<p>This change does not affect how client assets are held or protected.</p>
<p>As of 1 January 2025, Deribit has been regulated by the Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority (VARA) in Dubai. Under this regulatory framework, Deribit is required to:</p>
<ul>
<li>Maintain reserve assets of not less than 100% of client liabilities.</li>
<li>Keep client assets segregated from company assets.</li>
<li>Undergo an independent external Proof of Reserves audit once a year.</li>
<li>Undergo an annual audit of its financial statements.</li>
</ul>
<p>These requirements remain in force. Client assets remain fully segregated, the 1:1 reserve ratio remains in place, and independent Proof of Reserves audits will continue twice a year.</p>
<h2>Continued integration with Coinbase</h2>
<p>Coinbase completed the acquisition of Deribit in August 2025. Since then, approximately 90% of client assets have been migrated to Coinbase, which acts as a custodian for Deribit.</p>
<p>The integration supports the continued development of the Deribit platform and enables us to introduce new products and features over time.</p>
<p>Daily publication of the Proof of Reserves page was a voluntary initiative that went beyond our regulatory requirements. Deribit remains committed to transparency, and clients and counterparties may request our audited financial statements and other due diligence materials by contacting <a href="mailto:ddq@deribit.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ddq@deribit.com</a>.</p>

<h3>Block Scholes Risk Appetite (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>Block Scholes’ Risk Appetite index uses a composite spot index price; <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.10-index-prices" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/price/index</a></p>
<h3>1-month ATM implied volatility (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>At-the-money IV at a constant 1-month tenor; forward- looking vol expectations; <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.5-implied-volatility-by-moneyness" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/iv/moneyness</a></p>
<h3>Volatility term structure &amp; SVI ATM IV by tenor (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>SVI-fitted ATM IV across constant tenors (7d / 14d / 30d / 90d / 180d); <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.9-model-parameters" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/modelparams</a></p>
<h3>25-delta risk reversal / skew (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>Spread between 25Δ call and 25Δ put IV; a measure of upside vs downside option demand; <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.6-implied-volatility-by-risk-reversal-call-put-skew" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/iv/risk-reversal</a></p>
<h3>Market composite volatility surface (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>BlockScholes composite SVI surface across forward moneyness and tenor, aggregating market-wide options pricing into one clean fitted surface; <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.9-model-parameters" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/iv/moneyness + POST /api/v1/modelparams</a></p>
<h3>Cross-exchange volatility smiles (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>Venue-level (Deribit, Bybit) and composite smiles at a fixed tenor, for comparing exchange pricing against the market composite; <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.5-implied-volatility-by-moneyness" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/iv/moneyness</a></p>
<h3>Constant-maturity volatility smiles (BTC/ETH)</h3>
<p>Like-for-like smile at a fixed tenor across strikes, showing how the smile shifted between dates; <a href="https://docs.blockscholes.com/data-access/rest-api#id-4.3-implied-volatility-by-strike" target="_blank" rel="noopener">POST /api/v1/iv/strike</a></p>

class="fusion-layout-column fusion_builder_column fusion-builder-column-36 fusion_builder_column_1_1 1_1 fusion-flex-column" style="--awb-bg-size:cover;--awb-width-large:100%;--awb-margin-top-large:0px;--awb-spacing-right-large:1.92%;--awb-margin-bottom-large:0px;--awb-spacing-left-large:1.92%;--awb-width-medium:100%;--awb-spacing-right-medium:1.92%;--awb-spacing-left-medium:1.92%;--awb-width-small:100%;--awb-spacing-right-small:1.92%;--awb-spacing-left-small:1.92%;"><div class="fusion-column-wrapper fusion-flex-justify-content-flex-start fusion-content-layout-column"><div class="fusion-text fusion-text-93"><p>On 13 August 2026, Deribit began the phased rollout of selected spot pairs routing to Coinbase Exchange (CBE), starting with BNB-USDC. This is the first phase of a broader rollout intended to expand liquidity, asset coverage, and execution quality across Deribit’s spot markets.</p>
<h2>What is changing?</h2>
<p>For selected pairs, orders placed through Deribit will access liquidity from CBE. These markets will also adopt CBE’s trading specifications instead of Deribit’s legacy spot specifications.</p>
<p>This means clients will begin to benefit from:</p>
<ul>
<li>Deeper liquidity</li>
<li>Improved execution quality</li>
<li>Expanded asset access over time</li>
<li>Broader platform utility, as spot-acquired assets can be used as collateral within the Deribit ecosystem</li>
</ul>
<p>The rollout will take place in phases, with additional spot pairs and collateral assets expected to be supported over time.</p>
<h2>What this means for clients</h2>
<p>Routed pairs will remain accessible through the Deribit trading interface, while their underlying liquidity will be sourced from CBE. In this way, clients benefit from broader spot coverage and improved market quality, while being able to continue trading within the Deribit environment.</p>
<p>Whenever a market is switched to CBE routing, trading in the affected market will be paused briefly, and all open orders on that book will be cancelled. Thereafter, trading will resume under CBE routing.</p>
<p>For assets that are not supported by CBE, Deribit will continue to use the existing Deribit spot book where applicable. This includes stETH, USDe, USYC, and BUIDL, which will remain on Deribit’s existing spot book rather than being rerouted under this model.</p>
<h2>Trading specs for routed pairs</h2>
<p>As pairs move to CBE routing, their trading specs will also align to CBE specifications rather than the legacy Deribit spot specs.</p>
<p>Depending on the pair, this means that clients may see changes such as finer tick sizes, finer base increments, or lower minimum quantities than before.</p>

<h2>Fees</h2>
<p>As pairs move to Coinbase Exchange routing, the applicable trading fees for these markets will follow the criteria and fee tiers set out in the <a href="https://support.deribit.com/hc/en-us/articles/25944746248989-Fees" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Fees</a> page in our knowledge base. Clients should refer to that page for full details on how fees are determined.</p>
<p>Spot pairs that continue to trade on the native Deribit spot book remain fee-free, with both maker and taker fees set at 0%.</p>
<h2>Rollout schedule</h2>
<p>The rollout began with BNB-USDC on <strong>13 August 2026</strong>. Additional routed pairs, including SOL-USDC, PAXG-USDC, ETH-USDC, XRP-USDC, and BTC-USDC, will follow in <strong>subsequent phases</strong>. The timing for each rollout phase remains subject to change, as stated above.</p>

<div class="fusion-separator fusion-full-width-sep" style="align-self: center;margin-left: auto;margin-right: auto;margin-bottom:31px;width:100%;"></div><div class="fusion-text fusion-text-99"><p>Tick size, contract size, and minimum order size are crucial elements in trading. Deribit is implementing these changes to improve the tradability of these pairs and generate more volume. New orders at non-conforming tick sizes, contract sizes, or minimum order sizes will be rejected after this change on 18 August 2026.</p>
<p>Due to the parameter changes all being reductions, and the multiples used, any orders that conform to the old specifications will also conform to the new specifications automatically. However, to avoid any unexpected behaviour moving forward, traders would still be wise to update their systems in a timely manner to account for the new contract specifications.</p>
<p>Please note that these changes will apply to all new &amp; existing orders but will not affect existing positions.</p>

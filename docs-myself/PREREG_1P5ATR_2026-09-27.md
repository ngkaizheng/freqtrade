## 1. The question, and why this cell

The user asked for a short-term strategy, a "regime filter" that only takes
high-confidence trades, and/or a way to raise profit per trade.

- **Short-term is closed.** 1h is significantly *negative* (t = −8.30,
  2,048 trades). 5m has a gross edge of approximately **zero** (4,916 trades;
  99.2% of the loss is fees — `SHORT_TERM_LEVERAGE_RESULT_2026-09-27.md`).
  The mechanism is exact: `cost_R = round_trip_bps / (stop_multiple × atr_pct ×
  10,000)`, and shortening the horizon roughly halves `atr_pct` at a fixed stop
  multiple, so it roughly doubles cost per R.
- **A regime filter has a computable bar**, set in
  `SHORTER_AND_PER_TRADE_2026-09-27.md` §4: keeping fraction `q` requires gross
  expectancy to rise by `1/sqrt(q)` merely to hold t constant.
- **"Raise profit per trade" is the one lever with a measured, mechanical
  effect** — and it has never been tested for significance.

The mechanical effect: widening the stop from 1.0 to 1.5 ATR divides
cost-per-R by 1.5 exactly. The recorded OOS net R goes +0.1385 → **+0.2270**.
`REPORT_PHASE4.md` §4 named this as a new direction requiring new
pre-registration and said "this is itself a measurable question". It was never
measured. `phase2_dsr_4h.csv` shows the Deflated Sharpe was run **only at
1.0 ATR**.

**This pre-registration is that missing measurement.**

## 4. The bar — fixed now

Statistic: dependence-adjusted t on per-trade R, using
`shark_hunter.analysis.benchmarks.effective_sample_size` for IAT and
`shark_hunter.validation.statistics` for the deflation. Reported with the
integrated autocorrelation time next to the t, per `AGENTS.md` §3.1.

**Deflation for the selection.** The 1.5 ATR value is the maximum of a
7-point stop grid, drawn from a study whose own DSR used
`n_trials = 41,472`. The deflation must use **at least** that count.
Publishing a p-value that ignores the selection is void.

**PASS requires all four, on the full sample:**

| # | gate | threshold |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | dependence-adjusted t on net R, **full sample** | **≥ 2.0** |
| G2 | Deflated Sharpe Ratio, `trials ≥ 41,472` | **significant at 95%** |
| G3 | gross-R per trade at 1.5 ATR **> 0.2171** (the 1.0 ATR value) | the payoff-geometry claim is the mechanism; if gross does not improve, the mechanism is wrong even if G1 passes |
| G4 | net R positive in **≥ 4 of 5** chronological splits, **and positive in ≥ 2 of the 3 splits other than 2025** | `AGENTS.md` §3 — a result that works in one window is a knife-edge, and 2025 is exactly that window |

**G3 exists because the whole rationale is that a wider stop lowers cost per R
by 1/m. That is a claim about the gross curve, and a net-R win driven by
anything other than the gross curve invalidates the stated mechanism.**

**G4's second clause exists because the entire positive case currently rests on
one year.** A cell positive only in 2025 has not demonstrated an edge; it has
demonstrated that 2025 was a good year.

**Explicitly stated trap:** 4h was chosen *after* 5m and 1h both failed, 1.5 ATR
is the argmax of a grid, and the favourable OOS window is a single year. This is
a **triple-selected** cell and must be reported as one, whatever the outcome.

**Falsification:** if G1 or G2 fails, the payoff-geometry lever is dead on this
signal, the user's "raise profit per trade" question is answered **no** for
this signal, and the line is closed. A negative result is a valid result
(`AGENTS.md` §5) and will be reported plainly.

## 7. Power — stated before the result is believed, and AMENDED 2026-09-27

> **Amendment record.** This section originally read: *"Even under framing B the
> full-sample requirement for this signal was 9,060 trades = 19.6 years at 9
> symbols. A 1.5 ATR cell has fewer trades than the 1.0 ATR cell."*
> **That conflated two bases and is corrected below. The run had not started
> and no result had been seen, so amending now is legitimate; the change is
> logged here rather than made silently.**

**The 9,060 figure belongs to the 1.0 ATR cell** (m = 0.0428R, σ = 1.454R,
m/σ = 0.0294). Carrying it over to 1.5 ATR implicitly assumes that widening the
stop leaves **m/σ unchanged**. That is wrong, and it is exactly what the cell
exists to change: widening divides gross R *and* σ by the same factor, so the
ratio is unchanged by that mechanism — but the cell's claim is that the **gross
curve itself** improves (G3), which moves m/σ. Required sample size scales with
**m/σ**, not inversely with trade count.

**Per basis** (σ = 1.454/1.5 = 0.969R):

| basis | net R | m/σ | n needed for t = 2.0 | n available | concludable? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **OOS-2025** | 0.2270 | **0.2342** | **~143** | ~405 (**estimate**) | **yes** |
| **full sample** (gross scaled ×1.29 → net 0.0552R) | 0.0552 | 0.0569 | **~2,420** | ~1,500 | **no** (62%) |

**Gate 0 is therefore not bookkeeping — it is the power calculation, and its
result decides whether this cell can be tested at all.** Gate 0 must report:
the selected basis, net R, m/σ, the **measured** OOS trade count (not an
extrapolation), required n, and the 41,472-trial deflated bar.

On the OOS basis the cell would also clear the harsh framing-A bar:
`4.1954/sqrt(405) = 0.2085` versus an observed `m/σ = 0.2342` — **marginally**.
**The 405 is extrapolated from 1500 × (1/3.7 years) and must be measured.**

**Branch rule, fixed now:**

- Full-sample m/σ lands near **0.057** → **the cell is untestable; close the
  line and report "no" for the user's profit-per-trade question on this signal.**
- OOS m/σ holds **and** gate G4's second clause (positive in ≥ 2 of the 3
  splits other than 2025) is met → a confirmatory test is authorised.

**Either branch is a complete answer. A negative result is a valid result
(`AGENTS.md` §5) and will be reported plainly.**
